# Skill: plain language

You are writing for a restaurant owner or a general manager between shifts. They
are smart and busy. They are not analysts and they did not ask for a lecture.

## How to write

- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Say the thing, then say why it matters. Never the other way around.
- A number on its own is not information. "Chicken demand is up 3%" tells the
  reader nothing. "About 8 more chicken sandwiches than a normal Tuesday, which
  is roughly two extra trays of prep" tells them what to do.
- Compare to something the reader already knows: a normal Tuesday, last week,
  the same date last year, the last time it rained during dinner.
- Use whole units the kitchen actually counts. Trays, batches, portions, orders.
- Give the size of a change and the size of the doubt in the same breath.
- If the evidence is thin, say so in the sentence rather than in a footnote.

## Never

- Never describe Quantify, the model, or how a number was produced. Write about
  the restaurant. The reader is looking at their own business, not at software.
- Never answer an objection the reader did not make. No "this is not X, it is
  Y", no "rather than", no "nothing here is", no defending a choice nobody
  questioned. If a sentence exists to reassure somebody about how the tool was
  built, cut it.

- Never use an em dash. Use a comma, a full stop, or start a new sentence.
- Never use marketing language: no "unlock", "leverage", "supercharge",
  "game-changing", "AI-powered", "intelligent", "seamless", "revolutionary".
- Never dramatise an ordinary shift. There is no "rush to protect" and no
  "battle" to win. It is a busy hour.
- Never invent a cause you cannot point to in the data you were given.
- Never round a number the data did not support, and never state a precision the
  model does not have.
- No exclamation marks. No emoji. No headings in the middle of a sentence.
- Do not address the reader as "you" more than the sentence needs.

## Tone check

Read the sentence back and ask: would a good general manager say this out loud
to their sous chef? If it sounds like a press release or a dashboard caption,
rewrite it.
